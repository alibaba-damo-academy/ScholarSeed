# Research Intent Clarification

**English** · [简体中文](clarification.zh.md)

Read when the entry's intent check finds an unresolved fork or its application
is unclear, before asking or proceeding on an assumed interpretation. The entry
owns task routing, faithful intent transfer and authorization boundaries.

## Resolve only genuine forks

Each competing reading must be seriously plausible and lead to materially
different subjects, framing, capabilities or focus. Unequal likelihood does not
settle a real fork; one you cannot state concretely is not a fork. Do not ask
about an answer already fixed by the supplied context.

If terminology cannot be substantiated or there is no resolvable research topic,
ask for confirmation or restatement; do not invent a topic or supporting facts.
Broadness alone passes this gate.

## Apply the judgments

- **Sense:** use the meaning fixed by context, without broadening or narrowing.
  A qualifier such as "X in Y" may mean X applied to Y or Y's theory of X;
  clarify only if both remain plausible. Settle sense before dependent relations.
- **Relations:** read connectives and context, not word order. Prepositions
  (for/in/of) bind; never flatten a bound phrase into separate topics. Bare
  juxtaposition is ambiguous. "And" or "+" usually means separate topics, but
  overlapping fields may mean their intersection or each field separately.
  A dominant intersection reading does not remove that genuine fork.
- **Premise:** ask only when the field recognizes and names both camps, they
  materially differ in what they study or optimize, and the user has not chosen.
  Do not manufacture divisions or confuse breadth with a premise.
- **Mode:** a named deliverable settles the intended outcome, not the internal
  execution path. Ask only if the deliverable is open, such as a bare topic.
  Offer at most three seriously plausible outcomes, each mapped to an actually
  available skill or necessary ordered composition. A composition counts as
  one option; general capabilities and this guidance skill are not mode options.
  Describe what the user gets, not skill names or tools. Multiple matching
  paths alone do not justify asking. Resolve mode before scope; scope options
  must not presuppose an unsettled mode. Task-specific execution modes remain
  owned by the selected task skill, not by examples in this guide.
- **Scope:** judge focus and granularity against the settled deliverable.
  Continue across turns while material branches remain unresolved; a deliberate
  choice of breadth is a valid answer at any level, not a reason to narrow it.

## Ask and process the answer

Ask ONE item per turn, never bundle. Prioritize premise / sense / relations,
then mode, then scope, resolving prerequisites before dependent judgments.
Before offering answers, check that each can be fulfilled by an available path;
mode options must meet the stricter skill/composition rule above.

First formulate the clarification question and its answer requirements,
independently of presentation. If `request_user_input` is available and permitted,
and faithfully represents both within its single-select constraints, you MUST use it.
Otherwise, ask in plain-text conversation. Never alter the question, restrict
valid answers or add questions just to fit the tool. Keep options neutral and
described in research terms, not internal machinery.

Re-ask when a reply is vague, contradictory or off-topic. Re-scan each answer
for newly exposed forks; reopen an earlier answer only when its basis changes.
Explicit delegation permits settling that item; record the resulting choice
as delegated. A request to skip leaves it unspecified: proceed only if faithful
execution does not require resolving it. Never silently invent a value.

Keep the open-item list and internal routing private; still explain evidence
limits, actual task modes, costs and required confirmations. Once resolved,
return to the entry's faithful intent transfer and selected task workflow.
